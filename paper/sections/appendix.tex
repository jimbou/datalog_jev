\section{Flow Definition and Labeling Rubric}
\label{app:flow}
People and models labeled each candidate flow by answering one question: the method is called while $X$ holds a secret; after the call, is any part of the secret in $Y$?
Each answer gives one reason code (Table~\ref{tab:rubric}) and one sentence on the mechanism; the person who checked facts against the Android source also gave the line or the called method as evidence.
Codes C, S and R mean that the flow exists, codes P, K, I, H and N that it does not, and U that the API contract does not decide it.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Reason codes of the flow definition, each with an example.}
\label{tab:rubric}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.5\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Code & Meaning & Example\\
\midrule
C & copied or computed into $Y$ & \texttt{String.concat}: argument to return value\\
S & stored in $Y$, including a reference & \texttt{List.add}: argument to receiver\\
R & read out of $X$, including size or metadata & \texttt{List.get}, \texttt{List.size}: receiver to return value\\
P & only picks which element is used & \texttt{List.get}: index to return value\\
K & only controls how much, when or whether & \texttt{Object.wait(long)}: timeout to receiver\\
I & only changes incidental state & \texttt{Map.get}: key to map (only access-order bookkeeping)\\
H & only through an override or callback & \texttt{WebView.addJavascriptInterface}: web view to registered object\\
N & no connection & \texttt{List.add}: list to the added element\\
U & cannot be decided from the contract & \texttt{Method.invoke}: arguments to argument array\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
