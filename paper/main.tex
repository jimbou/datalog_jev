\title{Soundiness with Receipts: Certified Miss-Risk for Unsound Static Analyses}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{abstract}
Static taint analyses skip library code they cannot read and silently assume that no data flows through it.
A ``no leak'' verdict may therefore be wrong, and nobody says how likely that is.
We make the assumption visible and bound its cost.
We list every way data could pass through each skipped library method, the \emph{holes}.
For every clean verdict a min-cut finds its \emph{receipt}: the cheapest set of assumed-absent flows the verdict depends on.
A pricer gives each flow a probability of being real, and the receipt's prices add up to a bound on the chance that the verdict is wrong, without assuming that the flows are independent.
The threshold on that bound is calibrated on whole apps, with a fixed-sequence exact test, and checked on held-out apps.
On 324 Android apps, a sound over-approximation clears 72.0\% of the leak-free sinks; receipts clear 40\% of the remaining verdicts that depend on library holes at a 5\% target, with wrong clearances in 1.0\% of held-out apps.
Counting the assumptions without prices clears nothing.
The guarantee holds for every pricer we tried, which differ widely in power, and it survives replacing the answer key by the code-derived StubDroid summaries and by a human audit of the facts the clearances rest on.
On FlowDroid itself, removing its library model loses 86\% of the leaks it reports, which is the effect this work bounds.
We also report where the approach is weak: our analysis finds only two thirds of FlowDroid's leaks and over-reports heavily.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Practical static analyses are \emph{soundy}~\cite{soundiness}: sound apart from a list of features they deliberately do not model.
For taint analysis on Android the largest such feature is library code.
FlowDroid~\cite{flowdroid} models 349 hand-written library summaries; in our corpus, 12{,}274 of the 17{,}830 framework methods that apps call on a source-to-sink path have no model at all, and the analysis treats them as passing no data.
A verdict ``this sink never receives sensitive data'' is then conditional on all of those assumptions, and the tool reports it without qualification.
We measure the size of the effect on FlowDroid itself: with no library model, 54 of its 63 reported leaking sink methods (86\%) in 36 apps disappear.

The simplest remedy is to treat every unmodeled flow as real.
That is sound but clears only the verdicts whose derivation does not depend on a hole, 72.0\% of the leak-free sinks in our corpus, and gives no way to rank the rest.
The remedy of asking a language model for the missing summaries and using the answers as fact~\cite{iris,semtaint} gives no statement about how often it is wrong.
We take a third path.
We keep the unsound analysis but attach to each clean verdict (i) a \emph{receipt}, the minimal set of library flows it assumes absent, (ii) a bound on the probability that any of them is real, and (iii) an acceptance rule whose observed error on held-out apps is certified.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We claim no novelty for receipts as minimal assumption sets~\cite{bastani-oopsla15,zhu-aplas13}, for union bounds, or for the calibration procedure~\cite{lt}; the contribution is their combination for the unsoundness of an analysis, and the evidence:
\begin{itemize}
\item A formulation in which a clean verdict carries a checkable receipt and a distribution-free bound, and in which the guarantee is stated per program and certified by an exact test on held-out programs (Section~\ref{sec:approach}).
Verdict-level conformal selection does not hold here (realized false-discovery rate 7.9\% against 5\%, Section~\ref{sec:eval}); verdicts within an app share facts, and apps differ.
\item A proof that the min-cut is the tightest bound derivable from marginal probabilities when facts may be arbitrarily correlated, and a check that this is also the optimum of an LP over all worlds, which is what a Praline-style solver computes~\cite{praline}; and the observation that validity of the certified threshold does not depend on the bound being a union bound or on the prices being calibrated; they set power, not validity.
\item An evaluation on 324 F-Droid apps against four answer keys, including a code-derived key (StubDroid~\cite{stubdroid} on the Android implementation), a deletion benchmark with no language model in it, and an audit of the 446 library facts that all accepted verdicts rest on, partly by a person reading the source.
\item Negative and cautionary results: a general language model prices as well as or better than the specialized pricer we started with; our analysis is far less precise than FlowDroid and misses a third of its leaks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}
\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}
An analysis $A$ is given as a flow graph.
Nodes are program values and heap locations, a source node $s$, and sink nodes.
\emph{Code edges} $E_c$ are derived from code that is present.
\emph{Hole edges} $H$ are candidate library flows $x\to y$ between the arguments, receiver and return value of a framework method that has no body: for a call site of a library method $m$ with arguments $a_i$, receiver $r$ and return value $v$, one candidate for every pair of endpoints that could carry data.
The unsound analysis $A_0$ uses $E_c$ only.
A sink $t$ is \emph{clean} in $A_0$ if it is not reachable from $s$ in $E_c$; it is \emph{hole-dependent} if it is reachable in $E_c\cup H$.
Let $T\subseteq H$ be the true library flows, unknown to the analysis; the \emph{completed analysis} $A^*$ uses $E_c\cup T$.
A clean hole-dependent verdict is a \emph{miss} if $t$ is reachable in $E_c\cup T$.
Throughout, ``wrong clearance'' means a miss relative to the answer key's $T$.

\subsection{What counts as a flow}
\label{sec:flow}
A library flow from $X$ to $Y$ exists if, in ordinary executions with standard arguments, $X$'s data, a computed representation of it, or a reference to it reaches $Y$ through copying, storage or readout, including documented properties.
Mere selection or control (an index picks an element), incidental bookkeeping, and behavior that only a custom override or callback would produce do not count.
Cases that cannot be decided from the API contract remain unknown.
A negative label therefore means no transfer in this scope, not absence of every information flow; FlowDroid also has optional implicit-flow modes, which we do not use.
Keeping a reference to the argument (a queue, a retained listener) counts as a flow.

\subsection{Receipts}
A \emph{receipt} for a hole-dependent clean sink $t$ is a set $C\subseteq H$ such that every path from $s$ to $t$ in $E_c\cup H$ uses an edge of $C$ (a cut).
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cut}
If no edge of $C$ is in $T$, then $t$ is not reachable in $E_c\cup T$, so the verdict is not a miss.
\end{lemma}
\noindent The proof is immediate: any path in $E_c\cup T$ is a path in $E_c\cup H$ and would use an edge of $C$.
Receipts are small in practice: in our corpus, about 45\% of the clean hole-dependent sinks have a receipt of one fact and about 20\% of two.
They are checkable: each fact is a statement about a named library method (``argument~0 flows into the return value of \texttt{Boolean.valueOf}'') that a person or a test can judge.

\subsection{Pricing and the bound}
A \emph{pricer} assigns each hole edge $h$ a price $p(h)\in[0,1]$, intended as $\Pr[h\in T]$.
We use language models (Section~\ref{sec:impl}) and, as baselines, a frequency prior, a feature classifier and no pricing at all.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} and the union bound, $\Pr[\text{miss}]\le\min(1,\sum_{h\in C}p(h))$, without any independence assumption.
Because several cuts may exist, we take the one of least total price, which is a minimum cut with capacities $p$, computed by max-flow on the code edges (infinite capacity) and hole edges (capacity $p$).
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tight}
Among all joint distributions of $T$ with marginals $\Pr[h\in T]=p(h)$, the largest probability that $t$ is reachable in $E_c\cup T$ equals $\min(1,\mathrm{mincut}_p(t))$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For any cut $C$, reachability needs an edge of $C$, so the probability is at most $\sum_{C}p$; hence at most the minimum.
Conversely, decompose a maximum flow of value $F\le 1$ into paths with flows $f_k$, and let world $k$, of probability $f_k$, contain exactly the hole edges of path $k$ (padding with further edges up to the marginals, which flow conservation allows).
Every marginal is met and $t$ is reachable with probability at least $F$.\hfill$\square$

\noindent The min-cut is therefore the best bound that can be derived from marginals alone; a tighter bound needs knowledge of correlations, which the inference of Praline~\cite{praline} can use when it exists and which we do not have.
We checked the proposition numerically: solving the exact problem as a linear program over all $2^m$ worlds on 190 random graphs with 5 to 11 hole edges gives the max-flow value to within $2\cdot10^{-15}$, while the probability under independence is lower by 0.08 to 0.17 on average.

\subsection{Calibrating the acceptance threshold on whole programs}
A bound is only as good as the prices.
Instead of trusting them, we certify a threshold on the bound.
A verdict is \emph{accepted} at threshold $\delta$ if its bound is at most $\delta$.
For a program $P$ let $L(P,\delta)=1$ if some accepted verdict of $P$ is a miss, and $R(\delta)=\mathbb{E}\,L$, the probability that a new program has a wrong clearance.
$L$ is non-decreasing in $\delta$.
\begin{proposition}[Certified threshold]\label{prop:ltt}
Let $P_1,\dots,P_n$ be exchangeable with a new program, let $\delta_1<\dots<\delta_K$, and test $H_k:R(\delta_k)>\alpha$ in order with the exact binomial test of $\sum_i L(P_i,\delta_k)\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\alpha)$ at level $\varepsilon$.
Let $\hat\delta$ be the largest $\delta_k$ such that $H_1,\dots,H_k$ are all rejected (and abstain if $H_1$ is not).
Then $\Pr[R(\hat\delta)\le\alpha]\ge 1-\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent This is fixed-sequence testing in the Learn-then-Test framework~\cite{lt}: the $L(P_i,\delta_k)$ are independent Bernoulli$(R(\delta_k))$, each test is exact, and a fixed sequence controls the family-wise error.
Two remarks matter for the claims.
First, \emph{validity does not depend on how the bounds were computed}: any score, a union bound, a max, a product, would be valid after calibration; prices and the min-cut determine power and give each verdict an explanation, not validity (Section~\ref{sec:eval}, ablation).
Second, the unit must be the program.
Verdicts within one app share facts, and apps differ; conformal selection over verdicts with leave-one-app-out p-values~\cite{conformalselection} gave a realized false-discovery rate of 7.9\% at a 5\% target and 14.3\% at 10\%.

\subsection{What the guarantee says}
For a new app drawn like the calibration apps, with probability at least $1-\varepsilon$ over the calibration sample, the probability that an accepted clean verdict is a miss is at most $\alpha$, relative to the answer key and to the analysis $A_0$.
It does not say that $A_0$ finds the leaks FlowDroid finds, that the key is right, or that apps are exchangeable with apps of another kind (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
\paragraph{Analysis.}
Soot~\cite{soot} extracts facts from each APK (all shipped code, including bundled libraries); a Souffl\'e~\cite{souffle} program computes a whole-app, context-insensitive, type-filtered Andersen points-to analysis with field sensitivity and propagates taint from FlowDroid's default source list (84 sources, 193 sinks) with every hole on, restricted to the part that can still reach a sink.
Hole atoms at framework call sites come from a menu of endpoint pairs per method.
The relevant graph is stored compressed (a few MB per app), so every pricing, ablation and answer key is post-processing.
A min-cut is computed per sink site with max-flow on a graph in which each strongly connected group of infinite-capacity code edges is contracted once per app; the contraction leaves bounds and receipts unchanged (identical output on all 332 apps) and makes the evaluation about 25 times faster on the largest apps.

\paragraph{Pricers.}
Our main pricer is Jev, a model that returns calibrated probabilities for yes/no questions, asked once per candidate flow with the flow rule stated (``a tainted object taints everything read or obtained from it'').
Stating the rule matters: without it, getters on a tainted receiver are priced low and wrong clearances concentrate on a few widely bundled facts.
Baselines: counting (a flat price); a frequency prior over the package and endpoint kinds; a logistic classifier on method-signature features cross-fitted by method or by class; the language model's own yes/no taken as fact; and Sonnet~5 asked for a probability per flow.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
\paragraph{Corpus and keys.}
We built graphs for 332 of roughly 580 downloaded F-Droid apps that fit our memory and time limits (large Kotlin and Jetpack apps do not), of which 324 have hole-dependent verdicts: 84{,}753 sinks whose truth the key decides, 28{,}335 of them clean.
The \emph{main key} is FlowDroid's summaries, with Sonnet~5 labels for methods FlowDroid does not model.
Because that key contains language-model labels, we add: the \emph{deletion benchmark} (truth is FlowDroid's own models; a random share of modeled methods, or of whole classes, is deleted and becomes priced holes); \emph{K2}, StubDroid summaries generated from the Android~13 implementation (and OpenJDK~8 for \texttt{java.*}); and an \emph{audit} of the 446 library facts that all verdicts accepted at the chosen threshold rest on (AI-assisted passes, then a person reading the Android source for 67 facts).
Calibration uses 200 random half splits, $\varepsilon=0.1$; we report the share of held-out apps with a wrong clearance and the share of clean hole-dependent verdicts cleared.

\paragraph{RQ1: Is unsoundness pervasive?}
Of the 17{,}830 framework methods on source-to-sink paths, 12{,}274 are not modeled by FlowDroid.
Run on 40 apps with and without its library summaries (\texttt{-tw NONE}), FlowDroid reports 20 leaking sink methods in the 29 apps where neither run was cut short with summaries and 2 without; over 36 apps 54 of 63 (86\%) leaks vanish, and none appears only without the summaries.
This is the extreme case and measures how much a library model matters, not what FlowDroid misses with its default summaries.

\paragraph{RQ2: Does it clear more than a sound analysis, and does the guarantee hold?}
A sound over-approximation (every hole is a flow) clears 72.0\% of the 101{,}090 leak-free sinks with a non-constant argument; the remaining 28.0\% are the hard slice.

Counting the assumptions is unsafe even at one fact: clearing every receipt of size one gives a wrong clearance in 93\% of the apps.

The guarantee holds under every key (wrong clearances at most 2.5\%); the ranking of pricers does not.
On the deletion benchmark the feature classifier is strong when single methods are deleted (82--88\%, because it learns FlowDroid's conventions from sibling methods) and collapses when whole classes are removed (33\% at 75\%), the realistic case, since 86\% of the unmodeled methods that matter sit in classes with no modeled method.
A general language model asked for probabilities is the best or tied pricer under K2 and on the deletion keys; Jev leads only on the main key at $\alpha=0.05$ and is about 40 times cheaper (about \$0.4 against \$16 for the corpus).
Taking the language model's yes/no as fact has no knob: it clears 38--90\% depending on the key, with wrong clearances in 0 to 8\% of apps and a risk that cannot be chosen.

\paragraph{RQ4: How good is the answer key behind the clearances?}
All verdicts accepted at the chosen threshold rest on 446 distinct library facts.
A first, loosely worded AI pass disagreed with the key on 85 facts; a written, stricter rule (ordinary documented behavior, no custom overrides, influence is not a flow) resolved 86 of its 95 positives, and agreement with the key rose from 78\% to 97.7\%.
Because that rule was written after seeing the first pass, we state both.
A person then read the Android source for the 9 positives and the 18 undecided facts (all 9 confirmed; 11 of 18 became negative) and for 40 facts the AI called negative (12 chosen for impact, 28 at random): 4 were flows, all of the kind where the method keeps a reference to its argument, which we count as a flow; the estimated miss rate among the AI's negatives is about 11\% (95\% interval 2--28\%).
Calibrating under the key with all of these answers is identical to the main key to the last digit (39.4\% cleared, 2.0\% wrong), because every path through those facts also needs another candidate flow the key keeps absent.
K2 decides 176 of the 446 facts (45\% of their use), all as no flow, and agrees with the key on 173; it is not a perfect witness (it misses the prefix flow in \texttt{File.createTempFile}).

\paragraph{RQ5: How faithful is our analysis to FlowDroid?}
On 37 of 40 apps where FlowDroid gave an answer (17 found no leak, 8 stopped at its own memory limit), it reports 84 leaking sink methods; our analysis reaches 56 (67\%) with every hole on and 50 (60\%) under the key; excluding 7 sinks in FlowDroid's synthetic entry method, 73\%.
The misses are mostly inter-component sinks (\texttt{startActivityForResult}, \texttt{bindService}), file and network calls, and code in bundled libraries.
In the other direction we are far less precise: we flag 4{,}514 key-true sink methods and FlowDroid also reports 50 (1\%).
Our analysis treats all shipped code as live and does not model the component lifecycle.

\paragraph{Ablation: how the receipt's prices are combined.}
With the same cuts and the same calibration, the sum over cut edges clears 40.0\%, the sum over distinct facts 43.0\%, the largest single price 46.8\%, and the independence product 45.3\%, all with at most 1.9\% of held-out apps wrong.
Validity comes from the calibration, not from the union bound; the union bound buys an interpretation, and counting each fact once is valid and free.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\emph{Answer key.}
The main key contains language-model labels; K2 is code-derived but partial, uses FlowDroid's summaries for JDK leaf methods, and is not sound; the audit is AI-assisted except for 67 facts read by one person, and it checks the facts behind the clearances, not the rejected verdicts.
\emph{Exchangeability.}
Apps that bundle the same library are not independent; we have not yet calibrated per cluster of apps.
\emph{Corpus.}
Small F-Droid apps with half of the large ones failing to build; no Play Store apps.
\emph{The analysis.}
Section~\ref{sec:eval} shows it differs from FlowDroid in both directions; the guarantee is about library holes relative to our analysis.
\emph{Pricers.}
The pricers are language models or a classifier trained on FlowDroid's labels; Jev is proprietary and an open-weight configuration is not yet evaluated.
\emph{Definition.}
Results depend on the flow definition; we report the one choice that mattered (reference keeping) under both readings.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Minimal assumptions behind a verdict.}
Bastani et al.~\cite{bastani-oopsla15} compute min-cuts in a source-sink flow graph, each cut a set of statements assumed unreachable, and ask a developer to confirm them with tests; Zhu et al.~\cite{zhu-aplas13} abduce the library specifications needed to verify a source-sink property; Dillig et al.~\cite{dillig-pldi12} and Elrond~\cite{elrond} abduce proof obligations or specifications.
Our receipt is their cut with prices, and the human is replaced by a calibrated prior whose error is certified.
\paragraph{Library specification inference.}
Merlin~\cite{merlin} infers specifications with probabilities but thresholds them into facts; Seldon~\cite{seldon}, StubDroid~\cite{stubdroid}, Atlas~\cite{atlas} and Bastani et al.~\cite{bastani-popl15} produce specifications without a verdict-level error; LLM-based extraction~\cite{iris,semtaint} takes the model's answers as fact.
We use StubDroid as an independent key and the model-as-fact baseline in our evaluation.
\paragraph{Probabilistic inference.}
Praline~\cite{praline} computes sound probability bounds for Datalog outputs under partly known input correlations; on a reachability fragment with only marginals known, its optimum equals the min-cut (Proposition~\ref{prop:tight}), which we use because it is a max-flow.
\paragraph{Neuro-symbolic analysis.}
NeuroLog~\cite{neurolog} feeds model-extracted facts to Datalog to find bugs; we use the same kind of stack to certify the absence of missed flows.
\paragraph{Risk control.}
The calibration is Learn-then-Test~\cite{lt}; conformal selection~\cite{conformalselection} is the verdict-level alternative that fails here for lack of exchangeability.

\section{Conclusion}
Unsound static analyses can report not only that a sink looks clean, but on which library assumptions the verdict rests and, with a certified error rate over programs, how often clearing such verdicts is wrong.
The certificate holds for every pricer we tried; what a good pricer buys is power, and which pricer is best depends on how the answer key relates to the library code.
The honest limits are that our analysis is a weaker tool than FlowDroid and that the key is only partly independent.
