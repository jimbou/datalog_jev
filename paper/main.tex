\title{Soundiness with Receipts: Certified Miss-Risk for Unsound Static Analyses}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous}

\begin{abstract}
Static taint analyses skip library code they cannot read and assume that no data passes through it, so a ``no leak'' verdict may be wrong without the user knowing.
Current answers do not tell the user how likely this is: a sound analysis reports too many false alarms, library models written by hand or inferred by tools or language models can be wrong, and a list of the assumptions behind a verdict says nothing about how likely they are to fail.
We attach to each clean verdict a \emph{receipt}, the smallest set of library flows the verdict assumes absent, and let a language model price each flow.
The prices on a receipt bound the probability that the verdict is wrong, without assuming that library flows are independent, and this bound is the tightest possible from the individual prices.
A threshold on the bound is certified on a sample of programs, so that with high confidence a new program receives a wrong clean verdict with probability at most $\alpha$, whatever the quality of the prices.
On 324 Android apps, at a 5\% target, receipts clear 48\% of the leak-free sinks that a sound analysis cannot clear, with wrong clearances in 1.5\% of held-out apps; counting assumptions without prices clears nothing.
The guarantee holds under answer keys built from hand-written models, from library code, and from two language-model families that agree with a person's source-code checks.
Checking 25 well-chosen receipt facts raises the cleared share to 65\%, and a threshold certified on benign apps held on 31 malware apps.
\end{abstract}
